# Stakeholders

## Definition we are working to

A stakeholder is a group or an individual who is affected by, or is in some way accountable for,
the outcome of an undertaking. We split them in two:

- **Customers** — requested the product and will receive it.
- **Other interested parties** — provide broad constraints within which the customer's needs
  must be achieved, or have influence on the success of the system.

## 1. Customer

| Stakeholder | Expectation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The two of us, as the project team | An aircraft that completes a full V/STOL flight, and a record we are willing to show to a stranger | We requested it, we build it, we receive it, and we operate it |

**We are the only customer.** There is no supervisor and no examiner setting acceptance criteria
for JBV-01. Decision D1, logged in `NGO.md`.

Two consequences we should not hide from ourselves:

1. Normally the expectations are agreed with an outside customer, and that agreement is signed.
   Here the agreement is the two of us saying yes and writing it down. It happened on 2026-08-04
   and is logged in `NGO.md`.
2. Agreeing expectations with an outside customer is what normally stops the requirements from
   growing during the project. We do not have that brake. Nothing stops requirements creep here
   except the freeze and our own discipline. This is the single largest process risk in the
   project and it belongs in ConOps section 7.0.

We hold four roles at once: customer, technical team, project manager, and operator. When a
document says "the stakeholder", it is worth asking which of the four is meant.

## 2. Other interested parties

Ordered by how much they can change what we do.

| Stakeholder | What they expect from us | What we need from them | Influence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EASA** — Open Category rules, including the subcategory mass boundaries | Compliance. Not negotiable and already written down | Nothing. The rules are published | High |
| **RDW** — operator registration in the Netherlands | Registered operator, correct marking on the aircraft | Registration | High |
| **People near the test site** | Not endangered. Not disturbed more than is reasonable, mainly by noise | Nothing | Medium |
| **Readers of the portfolio** — future employers, reviewers | Reasoning that is visible, honest, and possible to follow without having been there | Nothing | Medium, but only at the end |
| **Suppliers** | A correct part number and a realistic delivery date | Stock, price, and honest lead times | Low |

### Notes on two of these

**The regulator is the easiest stakeholder to satisfy and the most expensive to ignore.** Its
requirements cannot be negotiated, and they are already written down where we can read them.
We recorded this at the start. It has not changed.

The EASA rules **have now been checked** against the regulation itself, and are recorded as
constraints C6 and C8 and C11 to C14 in `NGO.md`. Two things are still open: the subcategory
conditions in Annex Part A, which decide how far from people we must fly, and everything about
RDW registration. Neither page could be opened without a browser. Nothing about RDW in this file
should be read as having been checked.

**Readers of the portfolio are not customers.** They did not request the aircraft and they will
not receive it, so by the definition above they are an interested party. But they are the
audience for goal G3, so what they can follow is a real acceptance test, and it appears as MOE-2.

## 3. Primary stakeholders

The ones with the most influence over what we do:

1. The two of us — we set every criterion and there is no appeal
2. EASA and RDW — they set limits we cannot move
